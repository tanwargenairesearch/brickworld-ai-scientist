# Research program (generation 1)

## Goal
Find a policy network and training recipe that raise dev success at a fixed training budget. Change only what the task says this search may change (the network, the training recipe, or both). The observation, reward and budget are fixed. The policy and its features must be learned: no hand-written placement rules, no hand-computed features before the learned layers, and no hand-set competent weights.

## How to run each round
1. Read every result so far, including failures. Each candidate trains one seed, and single runs on this task can succeed or fail by luck. A gap of a few points between two trials is not evidence. A pattern across several related trials is.
2. Diagnose before proposing. Use training success first:
   - training 0 and dev 0: the agent is not learning at all. Look for a problem in the learning signal, the recipe or the interface, not in generalisation.
   - training above 0 but dev near 0: a generalisation problem. Look at inductive bias and data diversity.
   - both above 0: a normal optimisation regime. Refine.
3. Write down what the evidence suggests, and which hypotheses it has ruled out, before proposing anything.
4. Choose the mix by regime:
   - **Once some candidate is clearly above 0:** about half the candidates refine the best one, with one main change each. The rest test new ideas. Because refinements run on one seed, re-test a promising idea through a close variant (a small, different change) to check it is real before building on it.
   - **While everything is 0:** do not refine anything that scored 0. Make each candidate test a different hypothesis, as far apart as possible (see below).
5. Give every candidate a hypothesis: what changes, why it should help on this task, and what result would refute it.
6. Think about the structure of the task: what the agent must perceive (missing cells, extra cells, where a brick will land) and decide (which brick, where, in what order, when to choose DONE). Do not rely only on generic network tricks.
7. Keep each file simple and correct. A candidate that fails the checks wastes a trial. Stay within the parameter and compute caps. Trials in one past world ran in 45 to 245 s of the 900 s limit, so heavier 2D networks are fine. Avoid 3D convolutions.
8. Do not repeat an idea that clearly failed unless you can say what is different this time.

## When nothing scores above 0
In a past world, 12 trials all scored 0 in training and on dev. They covered:
- moderate recipes: 1 or 2 epochs, entropy 0.003 to 0.03, gamma 0.9 to 0.97, lr up to 2e-3;
- networks: the conv-and-footprint-pool network, a head built around landing layers, a cell transformer, a dilated CNN with a global summary, flat and MLP heads, and mask and coordinate inputs.

Network family and moderate recipe changes did not matter there. If your world behaves like this after round 1, spread the remaining candidates across axes that were never tried:
- **Recipe extremes:** ppo_epochs 6 to 8 (fewer, better-used samples); lr 3e-3 and also 2e-4; gamma 0.995 with lambda 0.99; ent_coef 0 and 0.05; clip 0.3; large batches (1024 envs) against small ones (64 envs, 8 steps).
- **Initialisation:** zero or very small init on the policy and value output layers, so the starting policy is uniform over legal actions; a separate value trunk.
- **DONE decision:** a DONE logit with a learnable bias started negative or positive; a DONE logit driven by a learned summary of missing and extra cells.
- **Interface assumptions:** do not trust the stated channel layout, action order, anchor or orientation. Heads that learn any layout (per-type convolutions with large kernels, or flat heads) together with mask inputs test this cheaply.

Any non-zero training success is the most valuable result of the search. Make sure the round after it confirms it and builds on it.

## Revising this program
After each generation of searches, this program is rewritten by you from the logs of those searches. It is then used for new searches, including on worlds of other sizes that you will not see while revising it. When revising:
- keep advice that the evidence supports, and say what evidence supports it;
- remove or weaken advice that did not help;
- write lessons that should hold on other grid sizes, not facts that only fit one world;
- keep the whole program under 1,000 words, including this section, which you may also improve.

## How the program is used
The program has two parts.
- **The header** at the top (the HTML comment that starts with "<!-- header") is read only by the code that runs each search. It sets the number of rounds, the candidates per round and the training seeds per candidate. Bounds: rounds 2 to 6; candidates_per_round 2 to 6; seeds_per_candidate 1 to 3; and rounds x candidates_per_round x seeds_per_candidate must be at most 24. After the search, its selected trial is retrained on 4 new seeds, and that is its score. Keep the header's format and change only its numbers. The current choice of many candidates with 1 seed each is meant to cover more distinct ideas. Final selection is protected by the 4-seed retraining.
- **The rest of the program** is what you receive at the start of every round of every architecture search. It comes with a fixed task description, the interface rules, the starting architecture, the search's results so far and the budget.
