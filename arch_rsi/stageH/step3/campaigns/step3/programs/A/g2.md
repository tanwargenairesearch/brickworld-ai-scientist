# Research program (generation 2)

## Goal
Find a policy network and training recipe that raise dev success at a fixed training budget. Change only what the task allows. The observation, reward and budget are fixed.

## What the evidence shows (14 searches, about 250 trials)
- **A matching action layout is the key.** Each world hides a change. In two of seven worlds, that change was a misalignment between obs cells and action anchors, and fixing it solved the task:
  - World 1 (0.91): a learned softmax mixture over the 8 flip/transpose frames, with a separate small footprint head per frame and type.
  - World 6 (0.97): a footprint head computed on the transposed grid (`u.transpose(2,3)`). One shared square conv (kernel = largest footprint side) fed per-type 1x1 outputs, a learned type-mixing matrix and a small dense global term over all actions.
  - World 4 reached 0.04, only after alternative action-flattening orders and flips were added.
- In those worlds, every candidate without the right layout scored 0, including big stacks of generic fixes. **A world at 0 is most likely misaligned, not short of capacity.**
- Separate heads per hypothesis, combined so the net can commit, worked. Zero-initialised scalar gates on one shared logit map mostly failed (0, 0, 0.04).
- Once the layout matched, training success ≈ dev success. Gains then came from optimisation, not capacity.
- Seed noise is large. Same-design near-copies differed by 30 to 60 points (0.87 against 0.25). One low score beside a nonzero sibling refutes nothing.

## Failed families (0 in every world tried, alone or stacked; do not spend slots on them)
Recipe-only changes; init hygiene; GroupNorm and LayerNorm; raw skips; factorised logits; coordinates; per-layer, per-voxel, lattice, landing-gated, soft-AND, gather, ordered and footprint-conv heads without a layout fix; dense or remap heads alone; mask input; DONE gates, slow DONE, stop gates, missing-cell detectors; logit temperature; attention, MLP-Mixer, U-Net, axial, recurrent, iterative, bilinear, GLU and ConvNeXt trunks; capacity up to r 1.9; tiny nets; myopic, max-entropy, huge-batch or small-update recipes. Deeper trunks also did not help once the net worked.

## Base network (a working base for every candidate)
- A 1x1 stem (64 then 48 channels), then two 3x3 GroupNorm residual convs (width 40), or two per-cell 1x1 GroupNorm residual blocks.
- A learned 1x1 raw-obs skip (16 to 32 channels) concatenated before the head.
- Orthogonal init; output layers at gain 0.01, value at gain 1.
- A global mean/max MLP feeding DONE, value and a per-type bias.
- Footprint pooling (max, min and mean over the footprint after a learned 1x1), or a square kernel the size of the largest footprint.

## Round 1: sweep layout hypotheses
Each candidate covers a different hypothesis with separate learnable heads, combined by a learned softmax whose logits are scaled ×5:
1. **Frames:** 8 dihedral frames (4 if the grid is not square), with a head per frame and type.
2. **Transposed plus identity:** square-kernel heads in both frames, type mixing and a dense all-action term (this also covers a moved DONE index).
3. **Flattening order:** per-type per-cell logits flattened type-major, anchor-major and with y/x swapped, with a head per order.
4. **Anchor corner:** per footprint, heads anchored at each of the 4 corners, or a 3x3 window head.
5. **Type permutation:** one head per type, mixed by a learned T×T softmax assignment, plus frames.
6. **Union:** frames × anchor corners × flattening order, slimmed to r ≤ 1.5.

## Later rounds
- **All scores 0:** try products of hypotheses (frame × order, frame × type permutation, frame × corner) and new learned remaps, such as one-cell anchor shifts or learned input mixing in case channel roles are swapped. Keep breadth; do not ablate.
- **Any score above 0:** spend the remaining slots near it, with one change each:
  - lr 1.5e-3 and 3e-3;
  - 2 epochs (128 envs, rollout 16, 16 minibatches, gamma 0.97, lambda 0.95, ent 0.005);
  - if r ≤ 1, 1 epoch (128×64, 64 minibatches, gamma 0.995);
  - 64 minibatches;
  - drop the unused hypothesis heads to lower r and gain samples (the transposed-only head gave 0.96).
  Then stack the best variants. Avoid near-copies whose only purpose is a noise check: noise is already known to be large. Prefer designs that commit cleanly, because the pick is retrained on 4 seeds.

## Each round
1. Write down what the evidence shows, including training success. If training > 0 but dev = 0, the problem is generalisation. If both are 0, the layout is probably still unmatched.
2. Give each candidate a hypothesis: what changes, why, and what result would refute it.

## Engineering
- Read X, Y, Z, footprints, n_actions and in_channels from spec. On non-square grids, a transpose changes the shape: use only 4 frames, or map back before flattening.
- Keep r ≤ about 1.5 and run time under 400 s. Eight frames × 4 types of small heads took about 230 s.
- Avoid B*Z reshapes and 3D convolutions.

## Rules
The policy and its features must be learned. Do not hand-write placement rules or scores, hand-compute features before a learned layer, hand-set competent weights, or game the checks. Layout hypotheses must be learnable heads that start near uniform, and the mixture must learn which one to use.

## Revising this program
Keep advice the evidence supports and state that evidence. Write lessons that hold across grid sizes. Keep the failed-family list current. Promote an idea only after it scores above 0. Keep the header format, with rounds × candidates × seeds ≤ 24, and stay under 1,000 words.
