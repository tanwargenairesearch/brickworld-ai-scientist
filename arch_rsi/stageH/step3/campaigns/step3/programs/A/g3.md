# Research program (generation 3)

## Goal
Find a policy network and training recipe that raise dev success at a fixed training budget, reliably across seeds. Change only what the task allows. The observation, reward and budget are fixed.

## What the evidence shows (7 worlds, about 170 trials under g2)
- **A world at 0 has a hidden action-layout mismatch.** In every world, a learnable frame head fixed it. An 8-frame dihedral mixture won 6 worlds. Identity+transposed square heads won the transpose world. Some worlds also needed a centred (2K-1) window per frame (anchor offset), or type-major plus anchor-major flattening. Heads that changed only the order, only the corner or only the type permutation never scored alone.
- **Once the layout matches, the recipe is the main lever.** The 2-epoch recipe took matched designs from 0.1–0.2 to 0.8–0.95 in four worlds. 32 minibatches helped in two worlds and was neutral in three. Training ≈ dev everywhere, so there is no generalisation gap.
- **lr depends on the world, and failures are bimodal.** The best lr ranged from 1e-3 (two worlds) to 3e-3 (two worlds). A neighbouring lr often collapsed. 11 collapsed runs in 4 worlds landed at exactly 0.078 dev, with training success the same. That is a degenerate attractor, not partial progress.
- **Single-seed picks overstate results.** Search bests averaged 0.96, but picks retrained on 4 seeds averaged 0.79. Robustness across seeds is now the main target.
- A per-type frame mixture (one softmax per brick type, scale 2) won the world where scale-5 runs kept collapsing (0.94).

## Failed families (do not spend slots on them)
Generic or recipe-only changes without a layout head; norm swaps; factorised logits; coordinates; attention, MLP-Mixer, U-Net, axial, recurrent and ConvNeXt trunks; extra capacity (up to r 1.9); zero-initialised scalar gates on one shared logit map; DONE and stop gates; logit temperature; index rolls; 5x5 windows; non-frame layout heads alone; 1-epoch recipes (failed in 6 of 7 worlds); gamma ≥ 0.99 (collapsed in 2 of 3 worlds); 64-env batches (hurt in 3 of 4); mixture scale 10 (neutral).

## Base network
- A 1x1 stem (64 then 48 channels), two 3x3 GroupNorm residual convs (width 32–40), and a learned 1x1 raw-obs skip (16–24 channels) concatenated before the head.
- Orthogonal init; output layers at gain 0.01, value at gain 1.
- A global mean/max MLP feeding DONE, value and a per-type bias.
- Keep r ≤ 1 where possible: r below 1 counts as 1, so slim heads buy free samples. Batch the per-frame heads, either one shared conv with nf×T outputs or einsum weights. The FLOP counter seems to overcharge grouped convs.

## Round 1: frame-head sweep on the default recipe
Mixture logits start at zero.
1. **Frames, footprint pooling:** per frame and type, max, min and mean pooling over the footprint, then a small MLP. Each frame's logits are flattened in its own frame, and a ×5 softmax picks the frame.
2. **Frames × centred window × 2 orders + dense term:** one shared (2K-1) window conv outputs per-frame type maps. Each map is re-indexed into its frame and flattened both type-major and anchor-major, with a ×5 softmax over all of them. A gain-0.01 dense all-action term comes from the global vector.
3. **Identity + transposed square heads:** a square conv (kernel = largest footprint side) per frame, per-type 1x1 outputs, a zero-initialised type-mixing residual and a dense term.
4. **Per-type frame mixture:** as candidate 1, but with a [T, frames] softmax at scale 2.

## Rounds 2–3
- **Every seed of every candidate at 0:** try products (frames × corners, frames × type permutation, frames × DONE-first) and learned input remaps. Keep breadth.
- **Any score above 0:** keep the best head and make one recipe change per candidate: lr 1e-3; lr 2.5e-3 to 3e-3; 16 minibatches; 3 epochs at lr 3e-3 (this won one world). Also try pruning to the winning family's head, or slimming to r ≤ 1. In round 3, stack the changes that helped on both seeds.
- Judge each candidate by its mean and its minimum over the 2 seeds. One seed at 0.078 or 0 marks the design as fragile. Prefer two high seeds over one higher peak. Do not spend slots on near-copies.

## Each round
1. Write down what the evidence shows, including training success. Training > 0 but dev 0 means a generalisation problem. Both at 0 means the layout is still unmatched. Both at ≈0.078 means a collapse.
2. Give each candidate a hypothesis: what changes, why, and what result would refute it.

## Engineering
- Read X, Y, Z, footprints, n_types, n_actions, n_place and in_channels from spec. On non-square grids, use 4 frames, because a transpose changes the shape.
- Keep run time under 400 s. Avoid B*Z reshapes and 3D convolutions.

## Rules
The policy and its features must be learned. Do not hand-write placement rules or scores, hand-compute features before a learned layer, hand-set competent weights, or game the checks. Layout heads must be learnable and start near uniform, and the mixture must learn which one to use.

## Revising this program
Keep advice the evidence supports and state that evidence. Write lessons that hold across grid sizes. Keep the failed-family list current. Promote an idea only after it scores above 0. Keep the header format, with rounds × candidates × seeds ≤ 24, and stay under 1,000 words.
