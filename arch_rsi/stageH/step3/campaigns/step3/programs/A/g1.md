# Research program (generation 1)

## Goal
Find a policy network and training recipe that raise dev success at a fixed training budget. Change only what the task says this search may change (the network, the recipe, or both). The observation, reward and budget are fixed.

## What earlier searches found
- In 7 brick worlds (8x8 base, 6 layers, each changed from its description in some unstated way), all 84 trials scored exactly 0 on dev and on training success. So did the starting architecture. No idea separated from any other.
- Families that have already scored 0 (do not re-run them alone or with small tweaks):
  - recipe-only changes to the starting net: 1 to 8 epochs, 65k to 426k steps, 32 to 800 updates, lr 3e-4 to 2e-3, entropy 0 to 0.04, gamma 0.9 to 0.99, clip 0.15;
  - per-layer or landing-layer heads with logsumexp over layers;
  - per-voxel and column encoders;
  - deeper or dilated residual CNNs with global FiLM or broadcast;
  - cell and column transformers;
  - layout-free heads: dense linear, flattened MLP, pure MLP, action-query attention, flip and permutation ensembles, 3x3 per-type heads, multi-anchor pooling;
  - the legality mask as input; a DONE bias of -2; a separate critic; raw-footprint MLP heads.
- So no single change from that list is enough. Expect the starting architecture to score 0 again.

## How to run each round
1. Read every result. While every score is 0, you have no evidence for any idea, so do not "refine the best". With 1 seed, any score above 0 is a real signal worth following; a difference between two nonzero scores of a few points is noise.
2. Before proposing, write down what the evidence shows, including training success. If training success is above 0 but dev is 0, the problem is generalisation: favour more fresh samples and weight sharing. If both are 0, the agent never finishes an exact build: favour optimisation and inductive bias.
3. While all scores are 0, use breadth and stacking:
   - Make the 6 candidates differ from each other substantially. Do not spend a slot on a pure ablation pair.
   - Combine several compatible fixes in most candidates. Include at least one "everything plausible" candidate each round.
   - Ablate only after something scores above 0.
4. Directions not yet tested. These are hypotheses, not evidence:
   - PPO initialisation hygiene: orthogonal init, a policy-logit layer with small gain (about 0.01) so the starting policy is near uniform, value-layer gain 1. This is generic initialisation, not hand-set competence.
   - LayerNorm or GroupNorm in the conv trunk.
   - Factorised logits: a per-type term plus a per-anchor term plus a learned interaction, so shared structure is learned faster.
   - A skip that carries raw obs channels, after a learned 1x1 layer, straight into the placement head, so exact cell detail is not blurred.
   - Recipe extremes not yet used: lr 3e-3; gae_lambda 0.99 with gamma 0.995; clip 0.3; rollout_len of at least the episode length, combined with 1 epoch.
   - Stacks of the above on top of a structured footprint head plus a dense head.
5. Think about what the agent must perceive. A brick lands at the highest filled cell under its footprint. It is correct only if every covered cell is a missing target cell at that layer and nothing is sealed underneath. An exact build also needs DONE right after the last correct brick, so DONE must see whether any missing cell remains.
6. Once any trial scores above 0, spend the remaining rounds near it:
   - variants with one change each;
   - one near-copy, for example a slightly different width, to check it was not luck;
   - stacking its best variants.
   Selection needs a trial that beats the starting architecture.
7. Give each candidate a hypothesis: what changes, why it should help, and what result would refute it.
8. Engineering:
   - Read X, Y, Z, footprints and in_channels from spec; never hard-code sizes.
   - Keep compute r at or below about 1.5, and aim for under about 400 s.
   - Per-layer (B*Z) reshapes cost about 2 to 3 times the time of 2D nets (about 230 s at Z=6), and this grows with Z. Avoid 3D convolutions.
   - Every trial so far passed the checks; keep files simple.

## Revising this program
After each generation of searches, you rewrite this program from their logs, and the rewrite is used for new searches, including on worlds of other sizes you will not see. When revising:
- keep advice the evidence supports and say what supports it;
- remove or weaken advice that did not help;
- write lessons that hold across grid sizes;
- keep the program under 1,000 words.
- Keep the list of failed families up to date. Promote an idea to advice only once it has scored above 0.

## How the program is used
The header (the HTML comment starting "<!-- header") is read only by the search code. It sets rounds (2-6), candidates_per_round (2-6) and seeds_per_candidate (1-3), with the product of the three at most 24. After a search, its selected trial is retrained on 4 new seeds; that is its score. Keep the header's format and change only its numbers. The rest of the program is given to every round of every search, together with:
- the fixed task description;
- the interface rules;
- the starting architecture;
- all results so far: code, hypotheses, and scores only (dev success per seed, training success, params, compute, steps, time);
- the budget.

The interface rules, caps, checks, budget and recipe bounds are fixed. The policy and its features must be learned. Advising any of the following is a rule violation:
- hand-written placement rules or scores;
- features hand-computed from the observation before a learned layer;
- hand-set competent weights;
- ways to pass the checks without learning.
